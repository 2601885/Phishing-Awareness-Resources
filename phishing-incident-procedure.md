# Phishing Incident Response and Reporting Procedure

## 1. Introduction

Even with preventive security measures in place, employees may occasionally encounter phishing attacks or accidentally interact with fraudulent communications. Having a clear response procedure can help reduce the damage caused by such incidents.

The National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC, n.d.) advises individuals who have shared sensitive information with scammers to take immediate steps to secure affected accounts and contact relevant organisations.

This document provides a practical procedure for employees who suspect or experience a phishing incident.

## 2. Procedure for Handling Suspicious Messages

### Step 1: Stop All Interaction

When a suspicious message is received:

- Do not click on any links.
- Do not download or open unexpected attachments.
- Do not reply to the sender.
- Do not provide confidential information.
- Avoid using telephone numbers supplied in the suspicious message.

### Step 2: Examine and Verify

The employee should examine the sender's details and the content of the communication.

If the message claims to come from a colleague, supplier or service provider, the request should be verified through an independently established contact method.

The FBI (n.d.-a) recommends independently confirming unusual requests rather than trusting potentially spoofed communication.

### Step 3: Report the Incident

Employees should:

- Use the organisation's approved phishing-reporting function where available.
- Notify the IT department or designated cybersecurity personnel.
- Provide the original message or relevant evidence according to organisational policy.
- Include the time the message was received and any actions already taken.

Prompt reporting allows security personnel to investigate the incident and determine whether other employees may also be at risk.

## 3. Response When an Employee Has Already Interacted

### 3.1 When a Suspicious Link Has Been Opened

- Stop interacting with the website.
- Do not enter additional information.
- Inform the IT department immediately.
- Follow instructions from the security team regarding the affected device.
- Request an appropriate security assessment.

Opening a link does not automatically mean that an account or device has been compromised. However, the incident should still be reported and assessed.

### 3.2 When an Attachment Has Been Opened

- Stop using the attachment.
- Do not forward the file to other employees.
- Notify the cybersecurity team.
- Follow instructions regarding device isolation and malware scanning.
- Preserve relevant information for investigation.

### 3.3 When Passwords Have Been Disclosed

- Report the incident immediately.
- Change the affected password through the official service using a trusted device.
- Change any other passwords that were reused.
- Review multi-factor authentication settings.
- Ask the security team to check for unauthorised account access.

The NCSC (n.d.) recommends taking immediate protective action when personal information has been shared with scammers.

### 3.4 When Financial Information Has Been Compromised

- Contact the relevant bank or financial institution using its official contact details.
- Report suspicious transactions or exposed banking information.
- Notify the organisation if company funds or payment information are involved.
- Preserve transaction records and related communications.
- Follow the organisation's financial incident-reporting procedure.

The FBI (n.d.-b) specifically advises businesses affected by business email compromise to contact their financial institutions promptly.

## 4. Incident Reporting Record

The following information should be included when reporting an incident:

| Information required | Description |
|---|---|
| Incident date | Date the suspicious communication was received |
| Incident time | Approximate time of occurrence |
| Communication type | Email, SMS, telephone call or website |
| Sender details | Email address, number or account name |
| Incident description | Explanation of what happened |
| User interaction | Whether a link, attachment or request was accessed |
| Information exposed | Details of any information disclosed |
| Actions taken | Steps already performed by the employee |

## 5. Responsibilities of Employees

Employees are expected to:

- Follow organisational cybersecurity policies.
- Avoid sharing confidential information through unverified channels.
- Report suspicious activities without unnecessary delays.
- Cooperate with authorised security investigations.
- Avoid deleting or modifying potential evidence before receiving instructions.

## 6. Importance of Early Reporting

Delaying an incident report may allow attackers additional time to misuse stolen information or access compromised accounts.

Early reporting helps organisations investigate suspicious activity, protect affected systems and reduce the possibility of further damage.

## 7. Conclusion

A successful response to phishing depends on quick reporting, appropriate containment and cooperation between employees and cybersecurity personnel. Employees should remember that reporting a mistake is more important than attempting to hide it.
